Project: Wasel

Twenty questions against a fictional Egyptian ride-hailing app — every Day 01-05 technique, on a schema built for a completely different business than Rawaj.

What you'll be able to do after this lesson

  • Apply every Day 01-05 technique to a fresh schema (cities, drivers, riders, vehicles, trips, ratings) that wasn't built with these exact questions in mind.
  • Spot a driver-supply gap with an anti-join, then confirm with a second query whether it's a real demand problem or a data problem.
  • Save a "top drivers by earnings" view and reuse it across later questions instead of repeating the JOIN chain.
  • Materialize a temp table once, then pull two different breakdowns from it without re-running the same filter twice.
  • Rank drivers within their own city with RANK(), and see exactly how it handles a real 3-way tie.

Randa runs operations analytics at Wasel, the ride-hailing app expanding across Egypt. Leadership has a fixed budget for driver incentives next quarter, and wants her recommendation on which cities actually need it.

Marketing also wants to know whether the promo codes they keep pushing are paying for themselves, and someone flagged a coverage gap in the last leadership meeting that needs a real answer, not a guess. There's no new SQL syntax in this project: it's Days 01-05 (filtering, aggregation, JOINs, CASE, anti-joins, subqueries, CTEs, views, temp tables, window functions) applied to a dataset that wasn't built with any of these exact questions in mind.

•The dataset

Wasel (واصل — "arrived") is a fictional Egyptian ride-hailing app. Riders and drivers meet at trips, the transactional core (6,000 rows, with separate pickup and dropoff cities since cross-city rides are common in Greater Cairo) — everything else exists to describe one side of that trip or the other:

driver_city_coverage is the one genuine many-to-many in this schema — every driver covers their own home city, and about a third of drivers based in Cairo, Giza, or Qalyubia (practically one metro area) also cover a neighboring one. ratings are deliberately incomplete: some completed trips have a rider-side rating, some have a driver-side rating, some have both, and some have neither (Q19 asks exactly how many).

Before you start

Databases/wasel.sqlite is already included in this repo — no download and no setup script needed. No MySQL server or .env either — project_07.ipynb connects with a relative path (sqlite:///../Databases/wasel.sqlite), same as every project day.

•Randa's list

She opens with the headline numbers — how big is the business, and where does it actually happen.

  1. Q1How many trips has Wasel completed since launch, and how many were cancelled or a no-show?
  2. Q2Which 5 cities have generated the most completed trips?
  3. Q3What's the average completed-trip fare, company-wide?

Two things marketing keeps asking about — worth checking before they become a bigger conversation.

  1. Q4Which promo codes have actually been redeemed, and how many times each?
  2. Q5Are surge-priced trips a big share of the business, or a rare event?

With the shape of the business established, she turns to the drivers — who's carrying it, and who isn't showing up at all.

  1. Q6Combine every completed trip with the driver who drove it, their vehicle, and the vehicle type.
  2. Q7Which drivers have never completed a single trip?
  3. Q8Bucket every driver into a 'top earner' (over 4,000 EGP total fares) or 'standard' tier.
  4. Q9Which cities see the highest share of cross-city trips?

Two precise questions that need reusable definitions, not one-off filters.

  3. Q12Which cities average a longer completed-trip distance than the network as a whole?
  4. Q13Materialize this quarter's completed trips into a temp table, then pull two different breakdowns from it.

Drivers again — but ranked and tracked over time, not just totaled.

•Reused patterns, worth knowing before you start

Two questions in the middle of the list lean on techniques you've already seen, applied slightly differently:

project_07.ipynb
DROP VIEW IF EXISTS top20_drivers; CREATE VIEW top20_drivers AS SELECT d.driver_id, d.driver_name, SUM(t.fare_amount) AS total_earnings FROM trips t JOIN drivers d ON t.driver_id = d.driver_id WHERE t.status = 'completed' GROUP BY d.driver_id, d.driver_name ORDER BY total_earnings DESC LIMIT 20;
A view, not a repeated query

Q17 (each top-earner's best day) filters against top20_drivers directly instead of recomputing "who's a top earner" from scratch — exactly the Day 04 payoff of naming a query once.

RANK() on a real tie

Q14 ranks drivers by trip count within their own city — Alexandria has a genuine 3-way tie at 39 trips each, and RANK() gives all three rank 4 and skips straight to rank 7 for the next driver, exactly the tie-handling behavior Day 05 covers.

Randa's answer

The incentive budget goes to Giza and Alexandria — Q9 and Q12 show their drivers doing more cross-city, longer-distance work for the same fare structure. The promo codes barely anyone redeems (Q4) get retired. And the "coverage gap" flagged in the meeting (Q20) turns out to be a one-driver staffing problem in Suez, not a demand problem — a very different fix than the one leadership was about to approve.
